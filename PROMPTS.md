# Google Flow prompts for Sakeenah

Free Flow gives **8-second clips** and about **two good generations a day**. Your film is 60
seconds. So: six short clips, generated in priority order, gaps covered by slowing footage
down in the edit rather than generating more.

---

## Do this first: one portrait, then animate it

The hardest problem is that his **face changes between clips**. Generating six separate
text-to-video clips gives you six different men, and judges notice.

So start with a single still image of him, then use **Frames to Video** (image-to-video) for
every clip, feeding that same image in each time. Same face throughout, and image generation
costs far less than video.

### The portrait prompt

```
Photorealistic portrait of an elderly Middle Eastern man, 67 years old, warm
and dignified. Short, neatly trimmed grey beard. Deep laughter lines around
kind dark brown eyes. Weathered olive skin. He wears a plain white ihram
garment draped over his left shoulder, leaving the right shoulder bare. A
small, discreet white wireless earbud sits in his right ear. Soft warm
late-afternoon light from the left. Shallow depth of field, blurred neutral
stone background. Shot on 85mm, documentary photography. Calm, gentle
expression. No text, no logos, no jewellery, no headwear.
```

Generate a few, pick the one whose face you like best, and **keep it**. Every clip below
starts from this image.

---

## The six clips, in the order to generate them

If you only get three done, get **1, 4 and 5**. Those are the ones the film cannot work
without.

---

### Clip 1 · walking and listening · covers 00:01–00:12 · **essential**

```
The man walks slowly along a shaded stone walkway in warm late-afternoon
light. He holds his head slightly tilted, listening to a voice in his right
ear. After a moment his expression settles into quiet reassurance and he
nods faintly, then continues walking with a little more confidence. Gentle
handheld camera tracking beside him at chest height, shallow depth of field.
Photorealistic documentary style, unhurried. No text, no logos, no signage,
no crowds.
```

*Covers 11 seconds with an 8-second clip: slow it to 0.72× in CapCut. He is walking, so the
slight slow motion reads as deliberate.*

---

### Clip 2 · his medicine · covers 00:12–00:20 · **nice to have**

```
The man stops walking, reaches into a small cloth pouch at his waist and
takes out a strip of tablets. He looks at it briefly, then presses one out
and takes it with a sip of water from a small bottle. Calm and practical,
not fussy. Warm daylight, shallow depth of field, static camera at chest
height. Photorealistic documentary style. No text, no logos, no labels
visible on the packaging.
```

*If you skip this one, reuse Clip 1 and let the caption carry the meaning.*

---

### Clip 3 · carried by the crowd · covers 00:20–00:29 · **nice to have**

```
The man is gently jostled sideways by a slow-moving crowd of pilgrims in
white, all seen softly out of focus around him. He looks around, briefly
uncertain of his direction. Then he tilts his head as if hearing something,
and turns deliberately to his right. Warm daylight, shallow depth of field
keeping only him sharp, handheld camera. Dignified, never panicked or
distressed. Photorealistic documentary style. No text, no logos, no
recognisable buildings.
```

*Slow to 0.9× to fill nine seconds.*

---

### Clip 4 · back with his group · covers 00:29–00:34 · **essential**

```
The man walks toward a small group of pilgrims in white waiting a short
distance ahead, softly out of focus. As he reaches them his shoulders drop
and relief spreads across his face. One of them turns and greets him
warmly. Warm late-afternoon light, shallow depth of field, gentle handheld
camera. Photorealistic documentary style. No text, no logos.
```

*Use only the last 5 seconds.*

---

### Clip 5 · the distress and the pause · covers 00:34–00:44 · **essential, spend your best credits here**

```
Close-up on the man's face. He slows to a stop. His breathing becomes
shallow and effortful and he raises one hand toward his chest to steady
himself. His eyes show discomfort and worry, never panic. He holds still,
listening intently to something in his ear, and his expression softens
very slightly as he begins to consider it. Warm daylight, very shallow
depth of field, subtle handheld camera slowly pushing in. Restrained and
dignified. Photorealistic documentary style. No text, no logos.
```

*This is the shot the whole film rests on. Regenerate it until the face is right — worry
without melodrama. Slow to 0.8× to fill ten seconds.*

---

### Clip 6 · he answers, and help comes · covers 00:44–01:00 · **essential**

```
Close-up on the man's face, still breathing carefully. He looks slightly
upward, gives a small tired nod and speaks a few quiet words. Relief begins
to reach his eyes. He lowers himself to sit where he stands. A younger man
in a high-visibility vest enters the frame from the right and crouches
beside him, placing a steadying hand on his shoulder. Warm daylight,
shallow depth of field, gentle handheld camera. Photorealistic documentary
style. No text, no logos.
```

*Sixteen seconds from an eight-second clip: slow to 0.6×, or split it — hold the nod, then
let the supervisor arrive. If the supervisor looks wrong, cut before he enters and end on the
man's face instead. That ending is arguably stronger anyway.*

## Rules that apply to every prompt

**Never ask for the Kaaba, the Grand Mosque, the Mataf, or a wide shot of pilgrims circling.**
Video models render sacred architecture wrong — wrong shape, invented levels, garbled detail —
and a botched Kaaba in front of a Jeddah panel is far worse than no Kaaba at all. Ihram,
warm light and a stone walkway carry Hajj perfectly well.

**Always include** `No text, no logos, no signage`. Generated signage is the fastest way to
make a clip look fake, and Arabic text in particular comes out as nonsense.

**Change one thing at a time.** If a clip is nearly right, resubmit it with a single word
changed. Rewriting the whole prompt makes it impossible to tell what helped.

**Generate audio off** if Flow offers the choice. You do not need it — Sakeenah's voice is in
the screen recording, and you record his two lines yourself. Audio generation also costs more
credits.

---

## If generation goes badly

Use the portrait. One strong still image with a slow push-in across the whole 60 seconds,
Sakeenah's voice over it, and the live dashboard beside it, is a perfectly respectable film —
and it reads as a deliberate style choice rather than a compromise. Judges have seen enough
mediocre AI video this year that restraint stands out.

---

## His two spoken lines — record these yourself

Phone voice memo, close to the mic, unhurried, a little breathless:

- at **00:34** — *"I'm so tired… I can't breathe."*
- at **00:44** — *"Yes… please help me."*
